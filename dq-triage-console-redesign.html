Interface proposal · built 16 Sep 2026

Triage Console Redesign

Eight nav entries become five. The data-element page goes. The scorecard gains the thing it was missing — click a failing check, see the actual bad rows. Cohorts becomes Triage, and the detail page stops being five tabs and becomes three blocks: what we think, what we're going by, what was decided.

Mock data: the committed fixture, latest run 2 Sep 2026 03:00 Every figure below is real Shipped — this page is the record of what was decided Flags restart at 1 on each screen

Colour, four ways

Indigo signal is selected and is what every screen below is drawn in. The other three are still live — click one and the whole page changes, in whichever theme you are viewing, so you can put them side by side before this is committed. The brightness is spent on the accent and the severity chips, because those are the two things that have to read at a glance; the neutral ground stays quiet in all four.

Drop-in for dq-app/dq_app/ui/theme.py — the constants below are what the selected option changes. Nothing else in that file moves.


  


  


  

    
What moves

    
The app currently has eight pages, three of which only make sense once you have
      selected something on another page. That is the density problem: the nav advertises
      eight destinations when there are really four, and two of the eight
      (Monitor detail, Detail) open empty-handed.

05
Untouched

    
— Register, Rules and Tables keep their current design.


  

    
Register is the audit artefact and already answers the hard half of the
      question — it lists the problems nobody actioned, and why. Rules is where a
      shadow rule gets promoted, which is the app's only other write. Tables keeps
      its inventory and gains nothing but a shorter name and a demoted detail page. Changing any of
      these is a separate conversation.

  


  


  

    
Decisions I made that you should overrule if they're wrong

    
    
      
  • Tables and Monitor detail survive. You didn't mention them.
        I shortened the name and demoted the detail page to a drill-down rather than cutting them —
        say the word and they go too, which would leave four nav entries.
    • 
      
  • CDE scoping stays. The page goes, the denominator doesn't.
        Removing the scoping as well would change every number on the scorecard from 20 checks to 34
        and is a different decision — one with a real argument on the other side.
    • 
      
  • Sample rows appear on the scorecard, not just in triage.
        That widens where PII-bearing samples are visible from one page to two. Same rows, same cap,
        same grants — but it is a widening, and it should be a deliberate one.
    • 
      
  • Problem titles are generated from the hypothesis. The
        model already writes the sentence; the queue takes its first clause. If you'd rather the
        title be a stored field, that's a fixture and schema change, not a UI one.
    • 
      
  • "Customer records affected" can only be a floor today.
        results.check_run stores a violation_count, not the keys behind it,
        and the runner caps violation_sample at 100 rows per check — so four of the
        failing checks are truncated and no exact distinct count exists. The tile reads
        ≥ 650 from the keys we hold. Three ways out, in increasing cost: leave it as a
        floor and label it; add a distinct_entity_count column to
        results.check_run (exact per check, still not summable across checks); or have
        the runner store a key sketch per check so the union is computable. The second is cheap and makes
        each check exact; only the third makes the headline exact. All three are spec
        changes — tell me which and I'll write it up before touching the app.
    • 
      
  • The estate tiles are unscoped, the score is not. Counting
        "tables monitored" over only the CDE-attached 20 would understate what actually runs. So the
        tiles cover all 34 checks and say so in their heading, while the 93.1% keeps its scope line.
        If you'd rather both used one denominator, that's a one-line change — and it means either
        the tiles shrink or the score changes.
